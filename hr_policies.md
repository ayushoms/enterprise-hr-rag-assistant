# HUMAN RESOURCES (HR) POLICY

**Organization Name:** Japanese XTY
**Effective Date:** [22/06/2025]
**Policy Owner:** Human Resources Department
**Approved By:** Management
**Version:** 1.0

## 1. Purpose

This HR Policy establishes clear guidelines for maintaining a professional, respectful, productive, healthy, and employee-friendly workplace. It defines the basic rules related to employment, working hours, attendance, leave, conduct, performance, workplace safety, employee well-being, and separation from the organization.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to all employees of the organization, including:

* Permanent employees
* Probationary employees
* Interns and trainees
* Contractual employees, where applicable
* Remote and hybrid employees, where applicable

## 3. Equal Employment Opportunity

The organization is committed to providing equal employment opportunities.

Employment-related decisions such as recruitment, compensation, training, promotion, and performance evaluation will be based on legitimate business requirements, qualifications, skills, experience, and performance.

Discrimination or harassment contrary to applicable law or company policy will not be tolerated.

## 4. Recruitment and Onboarding

All recruitment shall be conducted through an approved hiring process.

New employees must:

* Submit required employment and identity documents.
* Complete background/reference verification where applicable.
* Sign the appointment/employment agreement.
* Complete joining formalities and orientation.
* Read and acknowledge applicable company policies.
* Maintain confidentiality regarding company and client information.

## 5. Probation and Confirmation

* Newly appointed employees may be placed on probation for the period specified in their appointment letter.
* Performance, attendance, conduct, and suitability for the role may be evaluated during probation.
* The probation period may be extended where permitted and necessary.
* Confirmation of employment will be communicated according to the organization's employment process.

## 6. Working Hours

Standard office working hours will be determined by the organization and communicated to employees.

**Example:**

**Office Hours:** 9:30 AM – 6:30 PM
**Working Days:** Monday – Friday / Monday – Saturday, depending on the organization
**Lunch Break:** 30–60 minutes

Employees are expected to report to work on time and complete their required working hours.

Flexible, hybrid, or remote working arrangements may be provided depending on the employee's role and management approval.

## 7. Attendance and Punctuality

Employees must:

* Record attendance using the company's approved attendance system.
* Maintain punctuality and regular attendance.
* Inform their reporting manager in case of unexpected absence or delay.
* Obtain approval for planned leave.
* Avoid habitual late arrival, early departure, or unauthorized absence.

Repeated attendance violations may result in corrective or disciplinary action.

## 8. Leave Policy

Employees will be entitled to leave according to applicable law and the organization's approved leave structure.

Leave categories may include:

* Casual Leave
* Sick Leave
* Earned/Privilege Leave
* Maternity Leave
* Paternity Leave, where applicable
* Bereavement Leave
* Compensatory Off
* Public/National Holidays
* Leave Without Pay

Employees should apply for planned leave in advance through the approved process. Emergency or medical leave should be communicated to the reporting manager as soon as reasonably possible.

## 9. Employee Nap & Rest Policy During Office Hours

The organization recognizes that a short, controlled rest period may help employees manage fatigue and return to work refreshed. Therefore, employees may be permitted to take a **short power nap during office hours**, subject to the following conditions:

### 9.1 Permitted Duration

* Employees may take a power nap of **up to 20 minutes per working day**.
* The nap should normally be taken during the employee's designated break or another approved low-impact period.
* Employees must not extend their working break by repeatedly combining lunch, rest, and nap periods without authorization.

### 9.2 Permitted Time

A nap should preferably be taken during the afternoon, for example between **1:00 PM and 4:00 PM**, subject to workload and operational requirements.

Employees must ensure that the nap does not interfere with:

* Scheduled meetings
* Customer/client commitments
* Deadlines
* Team responsibilities
* Calls or appointments
* Critical operational activities

### 9.3 Designated Rest Area

Where facilities permit, the organization may provide a designated **Nap/Wellness/Quiet Zone**.

Employees should not sleep:

* At their work desk where it disrupts the workplace
* In meeting rooms without permission
* In customer-facing areas
* In unsafe or restricted areas

### 9.4 Manager Approval and Team Coordination

Departments may establish a simple scheduling or approval process to ensure that adequate staff remain available.

Managers should administer the policy consistently and should not unreasonably deny permitted rest periods when business requirements allow them.

### 9.5 Misuse of Nap Policy

The nap policy is intended for short restorative breaks and should not be treated as additional recreational time.

Repeatedly exceeding the permitted duration, missing meetings or responsibilities, or otherwise misusing the policy may result in withdrawal of the privilege and/or action under the company's attendance or conduct procedures.

### 9.6 Health and Safety

Employees who feel unusually tired, dizzy, or unwell should not rely solely on the nap policy. They should inform their manager and, where appropriate, seek medical assistance or take sick leave.

The organization may make reasonable adjustments to rest arrangements where required by applicable law or an approved workplace accommodation.

## 10. Meal and Rest Breaks

Employees will be provided meal and rest breaks in accordance with organizational rules and applicable law.

Employees should:

* Return to work on time after breaks.
* Maintain cleanliness in pantry and dining areas.
* Avoid disturbing colleagues during rest periods.
* Follow any departmental break schedules necessary for business continuity.

## 11. Work From Home / Hybrid Work

Where approved, employees working remotely must:

* Remain available during agreed working hours.
* Attend scheduled online meetings.
* Maintain appropriate productivity and communication.
* Protect confidential company information.
* Use company equipment responsibly.
* Maintain a reasonably safe and professional working environment.

Remote work is subject to the requirements of the employee's role and may be modified according to business needs.

## 12. Code of Conduct

Employees are expected to:

* Behave professionally and respectfully.
* Maintain integrity and honesty.
* Respect colleagues, customers, vendors, and visitors.
* Avoid abusive, threatening, discriminatory, or harassing behavior.
* Follow lawful and reasonable management instructions.
* Protect organizational property and information.
* Avoid conflicts of interest or disclose them when they arise.

## 13. Prevention of Harassment

The organization is committed to providing a workplace free from harassment.

Complaints should be reported through the organization's designated complaint mechanism and will be handled appropriately, confidentially to the extent reasonably possible, and in accordance with applicable law.

Where required, the organization will maintain specific policies and committees for prevention of sexual harassment and related workplace complaints.

## 14. Dress Code

Employees should maintain a clean, professional, and workplace-appropriate appearance.

Departments may establish specific dress or safety requirements depending on their work environment and client-facing responsibilities.

## 15. Confidentiality and Data Protection

Employees must protect confidential information belonging to the organization, its employees, customers, vendors, and business partners.

Employees must not:

* Share passwords or unauthorized access credentials.
* Send confidential documents to unauthorized persons.
* Store company information on unauthorized devices or platforms.
* Disclose business-sensitive information without permission.

Confidentiality obligations may continue after employment ends where required by contract or law.

## 16. Internet, Email and Social Media Usage

Company systems should primarily be used for legitimate business purposes.

Employees must not use organizational resources for:

* Illegal activities
* Harassment
* Unauthorized disclosure of information
* Excessive personal activities affecting productivity
* Downloading or distributing harmful or unauthorized software/content

Employees must also avoid representing personal opinions as official statements of the organization without authorization.

## 17. Company Assets

Company-provided laptops, mobile phones, ID cards, software accounts, documents, and other assets remain company property unless otherwise specified.

Employees must take reasonable care of company assets and return them upon request or separation from employment.

## 18. Performance Management

Employee performance may be evaluated periodically based on factors such as:

* Achievement of goals/KPIs
* Quality of work
* Productivity
* Attendance and reliability
* Communication
* Teamwork
* Initiative
* Professional conduct
* Role-specific competencies

Managers should provide constructive feedback and reasonable opportunities for improvement where performance does not meet expectations.

## 19. Learning and Development

The organization encourages employees to continuously develop their professional skills.

Depending on business requirements, employees may be provided:

* Internal training
* External training
* Workshops
* Mentorship
* Professional certifications
* Leadership development opportunities

## 20. Employee Wellness

The organization encourages employees to maintain a healthy work-life balance.

Wellness initiatives may include:

* Power-nap/rest facilities
* Flexible working arrangements
* Wellness activities
* Mental well-being resources
* Ergonomic workplace arrangements
* Regular breaks
* Employee engagement activities

Employees are encouraged to communicate reasonable workplace concerns to HR or their reporting manager.

## 21. Grievance Policy

Employees who have workplace concerns should normally raise them with:

**Step 1:** Reporting Manager
**Step 2:** HR Department
**Step 3:** Senior Management/Designated Grievance Authority

Complaints should be reviewed fairly, promptly, and without retaliation against employees who raise genuine concerns in good faith.

## 22. Disciplinary Action

Violation of company policies may result in appropriate corrective or disciplinary action depending on the seriousness and circumstances of the matter.

Actions may include:

1. Verbal counselling/warning
2. Written warning
3. Performance improvement or corrective action plan
4. Final warning
5. Suspension, where appropriate and lawful
6. Termination of employment in serious or repeated cases

The organization will follow applicable employment law and internal procedures when taking disciplinary action.

## 23. Resignation and Notice Period

Employees wishing to resign should submit a written resignation and serve the notice period specified in their employment terms, subject to applicable law and company policy.

Employees must complete:

* Proper handover of responsibilities
* Return of company property
* Clearance procedures
* Exit documentation
* Knowledge transfer, where required

## 24. Termination of Employment

Employment may be terminated in accordance with the employment agreement, company policy, and applicable law.

Final settlement, documentation, return of assets, and other separation requirements will be completed according to the organization's procedures.

## 25. Policy Amendments

The organization reserves the right to review, modify, add, or withdraw policies based on business requirements and changes in applicable law.

Employees will be informed of material policy changes through appropriate internal communication.
